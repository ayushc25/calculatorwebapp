About VitaCalc

VitaCalc is a small, deliberately unambitious website: nine health and fitness calculators, each on its own page, each doing one thing properly.

Why we built it

Health calculators on the open web tend to share three problems. They hide the formula, so you cannot tell whether the number means anything. They present estimates as facts, with no indication of the error bars. And an increasing number exist mainly to capture an email address or feed an advertising profile with data about your body.

We wanted the opposite of that. Every calculator here names the equation it uses, tells you what population it was derived from, and is explicit about where it stops being reliable. Nothing you type is transmitted anywhere.

How the calculations work

All nine calculators run as JavaScript in your browser. The formulas live in a single calculation module, kept deliberately separate from the interface code, so that each one is a pure function that can be tested against published reference values. Unit conversion happens before calculation — every formula receives canonical metric values regardless of which unit system you chose — and results are converted back for display.

Rounding is applied once, at the display step. Intermediate values keep full precision, so you will not see drift from rounding partway through.

Where the formulas come from

  • BMI — World Health Organization adult classification
  • BMR — Mifflin-St Jeor (1990), revised Harris-Benedict (Roza & Shizgal, 1984), Katch-McArdle
  • Ideal weight — Devine (1974), Robinson (1983), Miller (1983), Hamwi (1964)
  • Body fat — US Navy circumference method; Deurenberg BMI equation as cross-check
  • Calories burned — Compendium of Physical Activities (Ainsworth et al., 2011)
  • Pregnancy — Naegele’s rule with cycle-length adjustment
  • Period — last menstrual period with a 14-day luteal phase assumption
  • Bra size — region-specific band and cup conventions held in a data table

What this site is not

It is not a medical service, and nothing on it is a diagnosis or personalised advice. Every formula here is a population-level estimate. Estimates are useful — they give you a reference point and let you track change over time — but they describe averages, and you are not an average. Please read the health disclaimer, and take anything that concerns you to a qualified clinician.

Corrections

If you find a formula implemented incorrectly, a threshold that is out of date, or a result that disagrees with an authoritative source, we would genuinely like to know. Write to hello@vitacalc.in with the inputs you used and what you expected.
